# Coffee science reference notes

These are original paraphrases used to ground the generated narration; this is
not a recipe, health recommendation or measured extraction experiment.

| Narrated mechanism | Source | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roasting develops aroma through chemical changes, including Maillard chemistry; preparation conditions affect the cup. | [ACS, Chemistry of Tea and Coffee](https://axial.acs.org/agriculture-and-food-chemistry/chemistry-tea-coffee) | General chemistry; no invented roast temperatures or reaction counts. |
| Aroma compounds have different extraction behavior. | [Modeling Mass and Heat Transfer in Multiphase Coffee Aroma Extraction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.0c01153) | Supports differing kinetics; the animation is schematic, not a molecular simulation. |
| Finer grinding need not improve extraction when flow becomes uneven. | [Lee, Smith and Arshad, Uneven Extraction in Coffee Brewing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12373) | The paper models a possible mechanism; the script says can/possibility rather than claiming all brewers behave identically. |
| Beverage strength and extraction yield describe different properties. | [An Electrochemical Descriptor for Coffee Quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14950) | Background distinction; no inference that darkness alone measures extraction. |

The displayed dots, pores, water routes and soluble-material shapes are conceptual
illustrations. Their counts are not experimental quantities. Adding water is
shown after brewing: dilution changes concentration, not the past extraction.
