# Factual grounding and illustration boundaries

The script explains a common account of procrastination, not a universal cause
or a diagnosis. The character and their assignment are an invented everyday
illustration, explicitly described as such in the narration. Clouds, room
changes and the tilted desk represent discomfort; none depict brain activity.

| Claim | Research source | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avoiding an aversive intended task can prioritize immediate mood repair over later goals. | [Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12011) | An influential explanatory account; no claim that every delay has this cause. |
| Emotional avoidance can provide short-term relief while the task and later pressure remain. | [Pychyl & Sirois 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-802862-9.00008-6) | General mechanism, illustrated with an original phone/document scene. |
| Emotion-regulation difficulties are associated with academic procrastination. | [Emotion Regulation Difficulties and Academic Procrastination](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.524588/full) | Association is not proof of one universal cause. |
| Mood and tempting alternatives relate to procrastination. | [Giving in when feeling less good](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjso.12243) | No invented effect size or guarantee that a particular tactic works. |

The small first action is presented as a qualified practical implication, not a
treatment or promise. Starting is not guaranteed to remove discomfort or make
the task effortless. Rest and deliberate rescheduling are not automatically
procrastination. No invented dopamine, brain-region battle, experiment or statistic.
