# Short-form storytelling

Apply this editorial pass silently while drafting the requested title and script.
Return only the requested JSON. Do not output an outline, critique, or skill name.
The supplied intent mode, source fidelity, brainrot intensity, language, word limit,
and spoken format remain binding. This skill changes the telling, not those rules.
Write for ninety to one hundred eighty seconds including the spoken title and
pauses, aiming for roughly two minutes (280-380 words). Use substantive development
to fill that time; do not repeat the premise, invent factual details, or pad an
inadequate source story merely to reach the duration.

## Choose the story's engine

Find one concrete tension: a desire meeting an obstacle, a decision with a cost,
a small cause with a surprising consequence, or a mystery with an earned answer.
Choose the engine that fits this topic. An explanation can follow how a mechanism
changes someone's situation; it does not need a fabricated protagonist or villain.
For fiction, derive consequences from the supplied world's rules. For source
retellings, use only supplied events, motives, and outcomes; do not invent dialogue
or feelings. For factual subjects, omit unsupported details rather than adding
specificity through fabricated numbers, anecdotes, quotes, or evidence.

## Make a promise worth keeping

Silently consider three genuinely different openings, then use the one that best
fits the material. Choose from these approaches rather than always reversing a
claim: an immediate consequence, a revealing detail, a costly decision, a ticking
deadline, an unanswered mechanism, a small failure that spreads, a collision of
goals, or a scene already in motion. A grounded explanation can begin with a
mechanism or real consequence; a fictional premise can begin inside its crisis.
Never manufacture a statistic, quote, countdown, or personal experience to make a
pattern fit. Give preference to topic-specific stakes, a clear unanswered question,
and a line that reads comfortably aloud. Direct address and numerical details are
optional, not scoring requirements. Do not show the candidates or a numeric score.

Use the spoken title to establish the central tension. Let the opening sentence
add a concrete problem, consequence, or unanswered detail instead of repeating
the headline. Give enough context immediately to understand what could change.
Invite curiosity about a specific missing piece whose answer the ending provides.
Avoid empty promises such as 'you won't believe what happened' or 'watch until
the end'. A question is optional; do not turn every opening into a question,
debunking, or 'everyone thinks X, but actually Y' template.

## Advance instead of restating

Make each beat change the situation or deepen the explanation. Connect beats
through cause, obstacle, choice, and consequence rather than listing events.
Reveal useful information throughout; do not withhold the entire substance for
the last line. Use two or three meaningful developments when the material allows.
Escalate through consequences and stakes, not just louder adjectives or more slang.
Keep the listener oriented: clear subjects, concrete verbs, and no unexplained
pronoun jumps. Let a short sentence give an important reveal room to land.
Narration must carry the story alone over unrelated parkour footage; never rely
on a shot list, on-screen evidence, or an unseen visual to explain a crucial beat.

## Earn the ending

Resolve the opening's specific promise with an outcome, a clear causal insight,
or a final consequence. A callback works when the opening detail gains new meaning.
Use a twist only if supported by the source or fictional premise; never force one.
Stay inside fictional premises through the payoff. Factual narration can deliver
surprise through the real mechanism without pretending an unsupported claim is true.
Finish when the payoff lands. Cut the recap, generic moral, engagement request,
and any extra sentence explaining why the ending was clever.

## Final silent edit

Check the narrative promises: what does the opening lead the listener to expect,
which later beat changes their understanding, and which final line pays it off?
Check that events follow the world's established rules and characters' supplied
motives. A story can be absurd and internally coherent. Grounded topics need a
clear mechanism, not a forced hero's journey. Select the appropriate structure;
do not force a twist, thesis, or moral onto every topic. Resolve dangling promises
and remove contradictions before returning the final script. Keep this review
inside the existing generation, without a separate critic call or analysis output.

Read for the ear: cut setup that delays the point, repeated claims, filler bridges,
and jokes that obscure the next event. Prefer natural speech to polished essay
language. Pick a structure suited to this topic (consequence first, unfolding
incident, escalating problem, or a mechanism revealed) rather than forcing every
story into the same reversal. Do not promise variation across unseen previous jobs.
Check the title plus script against the requested word limit and make sure the
ending actually delivers what the opening invited the listener to discover.
